Greenwich Palace

Footprints come from Simon Thurley's three plans of Greenwich as it stood in 1547. Dates come from Historic England's scheduling entry and from Thurley. Heights and details come from the pictures listed below. Greenwich is thinly documented: much here is estimate. Click a building to read what is evidence and what is estimate.

Year

1547

The buildings are those of Thurley's plans of 1547. Their dates come mostly from Historic England's scheduling entry: Henry VII's house appears in 1505, the tiltyard and its towers in 1518, the Banquet House and Disguising House in 1527. Buildings that no source in hand dates are drawn faint before 1547. Demolition began in 1662 and its order is not recorded, so everything is removed that year. The Queen's House appears in 1635, sized from plans at All Souls College, Oxford, with its foundations from 1617; its position is an estimate. The castle on the hill stands until 1661 and is a ruin until 1675; the Royal Observatory stands on its site from 1676. Their position, much of their form and the hill are estimates. Other works recorded after 1547, Charles II's new palace among them, are noted here by year but not modelled: no plan in hand shows them.

    How to read this model
    • Where and when. Simon Thurley's plans are treated as ground truth for where each building stood and how big it was. The plan on the ground is his Plan 2, the block plan of the manor and gardens in 1547; it places the tiltyard, gardens and outlying buildings. The palace itself is placed from his Plan 3, the ground-floor plan of c. 1547, fitted to Plan 2 along the river front (to within about 1 m there). Where a picture disagrees with a plan, the plan wins.
    • Scale. 0.278 m per pixel of the plan on the ground, from the scale bar on Plan 3; the bar on Plan 2 gives 0.275. The photographs of the plans are not perfectly flat, and their scale bars disagree with one another by up to 3%, so every dimension is good to about 3%. The photograph of Plan 2 is blurred, so outlying buildings are placed to about 2 m.
    • What it looked like. Heights, roofs, windows and materials come from the pictures listed below, above all Thurley's reconstructed river elevation of 1509 and Wyngaerde's drawings of 1558. No source in hand gives a measured height: all heights are estimates. Where no picture shows a building, its whole appearance is an estimate; its card says so.
    • Dates. Most come from Historic England's scheduling entry for the site (2013), which summarises the building accounts and the excavations. Where it gives a span of years, the building appears in the year the work ended. Each card says where its date comes from.
    • Faint buildings have no date in the sources in hand. They are drawn faint before 1547, the date of the plans that confirm them.
    • The end. Historic England says demolition began in 1662 and that most of the palace came down in the 1660s, without giving the order; the royalpalaces.com entry says it was virtually demolished under the Commonwealth. The model follows Historic England and removes everything in 1662.
    • Estimated years. Thurley's first-floor plan dates some rooms only by decade (“1530's”, “1540's”). The model has to pick a year, and uses the middle of the decade; the card says so.
    • Ground cover is an estimate, except the tiltyard's sand, which Historic England records: sand in the tiltyard, grass and gravel in the gardens, trees in the orchard, gravel in the Inner Court.
    • The Thames. The Tide control raises and lowers the river. No source in hand gives river or ground levels at Greenwich: the levels are placeholders carried over from the Whitehall model.
    • Names on the labels and cards follow the year: the Friars' Church becomes the Armoury.
    • Thurley's detailed plans (ground floor and first floor, c. 1547) can be laid on the ground under Layers. Solid black walls on the ground-floor plan are the ones he marks as found; dashed lines are conjectural.

    Historical Images

    The plans and pictures up to Hollar's view of 1637, and the excavation photograph, are photographed from Thurley's book, listed below; captions give his figure numbers.

Baynard’s Castle

A riverside house in the City of London: rebuilt after a fire in 1428, remade by Henry VII about 1501, enlarged to the west about 1550 and burned in the Great Fire of 1666. Footprints come from Simon Thurley’s conjectural plan of about 1540; the phases come from the Museum of London’s records of the excavations of 1972–81; the river front follows Wyngaerde and Hollar. The house is thinly documented: heights, materials and most details are estimates, and each card says which.

Year

1540

Three phases. 1428–1500: the four-winged house with two corner towers that the excavations record, drawn faint because nothing shows it above ground. About 1501: Henry VII’s river front of five towers and a watergate. About 1550 (shown from 1551): the west extension, whose footprint is an estimate, since no plan of it was available. The house burned in September 1666 and is gone from 1667, apart from one tower.

    How to read this model
    • Where. Simon Thurley's plan (fig. 53 in The Royal Palaces of Tudor England), a conjectural plan of about 1540, is treated as ground truth for where the main house, the garden and the neighbours stood and how big they were.
    • When. The phases come from the Museum of London's summaries of the excavations of 1972–81 (Schofield and Maloney 1998): the house of 1428, Henry VII's river towers, and the west extension of about 1550. Stow gives the fire of 1428 and the rebuilding of about 1501.
    • The west extension has no plan. The excavation summaries say it had three brick wings, stone-faced to the river; the model lays them round Thurley's garden. That footprint is an estimate, and so the “plan decides” rule cannot be applied to it.
    • What it looked like. Only the river front has pictures: Wyngaerde (about 1550) and Hollar (1647). Everything else about the appearance of the house is an estimate. Each card has a “From the sources” part and an “Estimate” part.
    • Names. Thurley's plan names only the Garden, the Court and the Watergate. “West range”, “River range” and the like are descriptions made for this model.
    • Faint buildings are those whose date or form is not established: the house before 1501 (it stood, but nothing shows its form), and the garden and the two neighbours before the plan's date of about 1540.
    • Scale. About 0.158 m to a pixel of the plan photograph, measured from the plan's own metre bar; good to about 3%, and less good at the east edge, where the photographed page curls.
    • The plan on the ground is a photograph of the printed plan. Under Layers it can be faded out, and the estimated ground surfaces faded in.
    • The Thames is tidal. The Tide control raises and lowers the river. The ground and tide levels are estimates: no source used gives levels for this site.

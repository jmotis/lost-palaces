Oatlands Palace

Positions, footprints and dates follow the phase plans in the excavation monograph, Rob Poulton's Excavations at Oatlands Palace (2010, with Alan Cook and Simon Thurley). Heights and details come from the views listed below. Click a building to read what is evidence and what is estimate.

Year

1547

The model opens in 1537 with the Reads' moated house. Buildings appear at the dates the monograph gives. Where it gives only a span of years, the building is drawn faint from the start of the span and solid from its end, or from the first view that shows it. Names on the labels and cards change with the year. The model ends in 1650, the year of the demolition, with the palace standing.

    How to read this model
    • Where and when. The block plans in the excavation monograph (figs 132, 136, 137 and 139) are treated as ground truth for where each building stood, how big it was and which phase it belongs to. They are all at one scale; the model's scale, 0.151 m per plan pixel, is measured from their 40 m scale bar. Footprints were read off the page to about a metre.
    • How firm the plans are. The outer and middle courts and the gatehouse range of the privy court were excavated. The rest of the privy court, the galleries and the gardens were not: the monograph calls its plan there “informed conjecture” from the views and documents. Each card says which applies.
    • Compass. The model uses the monograph's words: the entrance front is “west”, the privy court “east”, the queen's side “north” and the king's side “south”. Its north arrow shows true north nearer the monograph's north-west; the compass in the corner follows the arrow.
    • What it looked like. Heights, roofs, gables, windows and stacks come from Wyngaerde's view of 1559, the Gough drawing of about 1600 and van Somer's painting of 1617. They are estimates; the monograph gives few heights.
    • Faint buildings are those the sources date only to a span of years, in the years before the span ends.
    • Where sources disagree. The monograph is later than Thurley's book of 1993 and corrects it on where the royal lodgings were. It also differs from the Royal Palaces web page on where the prospect tower stood. The model follows the monograph and the cards say so.
    • Not modelled. Interiors; the Reads' house before 1537 in its earlier stages; buildings the documents name but nobody has located (the chapel of 1537–8, the coal house, buttery and storehouse of the 1580s and 1590s, the bakehouse of 1603, the privy kitchen of 1616, the silkworm house and kitchen garden).
    • Phase plan. Under Layers, the monograph's plan for the current year can be laid on the ground. Black is new in that phase, grey is retained.

    Historical Images

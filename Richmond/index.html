A 3D Model, 1445–1703

Richmond Palace

Footprints are traced from Simon Thurley's plan of Richmond (Plan 11 in The Royal Palaces of Tudor England). Heights and details come from the views listed below, above all Wyngaerde's drawings of c. 1558–62, and from the Parliamentary Survey of 1649. Richmond is thinly documented: much here is estimate. Click a building to see what is evidence and what is estimate.

Year

1560

The model runs from the royal house of Sheen about 1445 to 1703. Before 1498 no plan survives in the sources read: only the privy lodgings are shown, faint. 1498–1500: the palace is being rebuilt after the fire of December 1497; every building is faint because the order of building is not known. 1501–1649: the whole palace as Thurley's Plan 11 lays it out (the plan itself is undated). 1650–59: the stone buildings are faint while they are being pulled down. From 1660: the brick ranges and the Middle Gate stand; buildings whose fate is not recorded are faint. Faint always means “not established”.

    How to read this model
    • Where. Simon Thurley's Plan 11 is treated as ground truth for where each building stood and how big it was. Where a picture disagrees with it, the plan wins. The plan was photographed from the book and used without correcting the camera angle: long walls on it were checked and run true to within about a fifth of a degree, but sizes may be out by a few per cent.
    • When. Plan 11 carries no date. The dates come from the Victoria County History (fire December 1497, rebuilding complete 1501, friars 1499–1534, sale 1650, dismantled by 1660), Thurley's text (rebuilding in two stages, the main one to about 1501; his account of the second stage has not been read), and the Richmond Local History Notes (gate storey of the 1590s, demolitions of the 1650s, Trumpeters' House 1702–3). The sources disagree about the fire: Lyttelton gives 21 December 1498. The model follows 1497.
    • What it looked like. Heights, roofs, windows and materials come from the views listed below, read by eye. Where no view shows a building, its appearance is an estimate; its card says so. All heights are estimates.
    • Faint buildings. A building is drawn faint in years when the sources read do not establish its state: 1498–1500, while the palace was being built in an unknown order; the 1650s, for the stone buildings being demolished; and from 1660, for buildings whose fate is not recorded. For most buildings “standing from 1501” is itself an estimate, since only the hall has a date of its own; each card says so.
    • The 1649 survey describes the palace as it stood 150 years after it was built. Its storeys, materials and roofs are applied to the whole life of each building; later alterations it may reflect cannot be told apart. Its measurements run a little larger than the same buildings measured off Plan 11 (hall 100 by 40 ft against about 95 by 40; inner court 67 by 66 ft against about 64 by 60); the plan is followed.
    • Unlabelled buildings. Plan 11 draws several buildings without naming them. They are modelled from their outlines and named by where they stand, with “unlabelled” in the name.
    • Ground cover. The surfaces are a separate layer and are estimates, except the inner court, which a contemporary description quoted by Thurley and the 1649 survey both say was paved with freestone. Orchards and the Green are shown as grass, courts as gravel. The orchards hold the numbers of trees the 1649 survey counts (223 and 39), set out on a grid that is not evidence; the round knot and yew in the Privy Garden are from the same survey. Under Layers, raise “Plan on the ground” and lower “Ground cover” to see the plan.
    • The moat and the river. Their outlines are from Plan 11; the far bank of the river is not on the plan and is drawn about 100 m away as a guess. Their depth, the height of the ground above the water and the tide levels are not in the sources and are estimates, there only so that the Tide control has something to show.
    • Thurley's plan can be laid over the ground in red under the Layers settings. Some walls on it are drawn in heavy line; the photographed page does not explain what the heavy line means.

    Historical Images

    The ground plan under this model is Simon Thurley's Plan 11, “Richmond, Surrey. Layout of the manor and gardens”, photographed from the book. North is at the top. Scale is about 0.205 m per plan pixel, measured from the plan's own scale bar (250 ft = 372.5 px); allow a few per cent, because the page was photographed and not scanned.
